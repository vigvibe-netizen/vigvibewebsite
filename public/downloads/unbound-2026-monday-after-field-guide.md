# The Monday-After Field Guide

Scott Vigneault / VigVibe

## Start with this prompt

Help me work through this guide one decision at a time. Ask about my marketing and creative team before suggesting solutions. Ask one question at a time and wait for my answer. Help identify whether the bottleneck is the brief, production, feedback or approval. Distinguish repeatable AI-assisted tasks from human creative judgment. Finish with one practical experiment, a success measure, a clear AI/human handoff and the points where a creative director or senior designer could help facilitate the work. Do not invent conference quotes or assume tools my team has not confirmed.

## 01 / NOTICE: What deserves to change?

Start with an idea from your UNBOUND notes that could change a real decision for your team.

- What does Marketing want to do that Creative cannot currently support: more campaign variations, faster testing, or better use of existing content?
- Would this idea improve the work, or just increase the volume?

Write: We want to ___, but today we cannot because ___.

Where creative leadership helps: Experienced creative direction turns a marketing ambition into a focused creative opportunity.

Next: Take that ambition to page 2. Find the handoff that holds it back.

## 02 / LOCATE: Where does the work get stuck?

A production problem may actually be an unclear brief, conflicting feedback or a missing decision.

- Does Creative have a clear audience, message, deliverable list and definition of success?
- Do revisions fix execution, or reveal that stakeholders never agreed on the direction?
- Is the delay in making the work, collecting feedback or getting approval?

Write: The work slows down between ___ and ___ because ___.

Where creative leadership helps: A creative director or senior designer can trace the friction to its source and help the team agree on what needs to change.

Next: Take that bottleneck to page 3. Separate repeatable work from judgment.

## 03 / ASSIGN: What can AI take off the team's plate?

Use AI for a defined task inside a clear process. Keep a person accountable for the output.

- Could AI turn scattered requests into a first-pass brief and flag missing information?
- Could it consolidate feedback, identify conflicting comments or draft variations from an approved concept?
- Who owns strategy, art direction, factual accuracy and final approval?

Write: AI will help with ___. A person will review it for ___.

Where creative leadership helps: Experienced creative leadership defines the handoff and judges whether the output serves the audience, the brief and the brand.

Next: Take that handoff to page 4. Test it on one real piece of work.

## 04 / TEST: What would make next week easier?

One small experiment gives the team something concrete to learn from.

- Try one shared brief before Creative starts.
- Consolidate stakeholder feedback into one agreed revision list.
- Create useful variations from one approved campaign direction. Measure revision rounds, approval time or usable variations.

Write: For the next ___, we will try ___ and measure ___.

Where creative leadership helps: A creative director or senior designer can shape a useful experiment and define what good work looks like before production starts.

Next: Take that experiment to page 5. Set the boundaries before it grows.

## 05 / PROTECT: What makes faster work still good work?

Clear standards let people move faster without renegotiating quality every time.

- What approved brand examples, messages and templates should AI work from?
- Who resolves conflicting feedback?
- What facts, rights and brand standards must a person check before anything reaches a customer?

Write: Nothing ships until ___ checks ___.

Where creative leadership helps: Creative leadership establishes practical standards and review points so more output still looks and sounds like your brand.

Next: Take those standards to page 6. Make something the team can use.

## 06 / MAKE: What will you put in front of the team?

Make something real enough that the team can react to it, use it and improve it.

- An improved brief, an approved concept with variations, or a feedback process everyone can follow?
- What will you make, who owns it, and when will the team review it?

Write: By ___, we will make ___ so the team can decide ___.

Where creative leadership helps: You do not have to facilitate this alone. A creative director or senior designer can help turn competing priorities into clear direction and usable work.

Next: Bring one stalled campaign, unclear brief or recurring revision problem to VigVibe.
