The question comes up most often when a marketing team has one stuck project and a budget
line that could go three ways. It gets answered by whoever is loudest in the meeting, which
is usually wrong.

Three things actually decide it: how often the work recurs, how senior the judgment needs to
be, and who owns the deadline. Run those in order and the answer is usually obvious in about
ten minutes.

## Does the work recur?

If you need the same thing every week — social graphics, sales one-pagers, email templates —
that is a headcount or a retainer, not a project. If it is a launch, a keynote, a rebrand, or
a report, that is project work and a freelancer is the cheapest path to senior quality.

## How senior does the judgment need to be?

An agency sells you a team, but the senior person is often on the pitch and off the project
by week two. A freelance creative director is the same person start to finish. For work that
has to survive an executive review, continuity matters more than headcount.

## Who owns the deadline?

If the deadline is yours and it cannot move, you want the shortest path between the decision
and the file. Every layer between those two things is a day.
